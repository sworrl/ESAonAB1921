SAVESTATE

Save your games. Tell the State.

California's AB 1921 makes "buy" mean something for games again: 60 days of notice before a shutdown, then a standalone build, a patch, or your money back. The ESA is lobbying to kill it. The senators who vote next are listed below, and this page helps you write to them in your own words.

        Questions people actually ask

        What happens to my data?

        By default, none of it leaves your machine. This site is static files: no database, no analytics, no cookies. Your letter exists in your browser and in whatever you do with it: your email app, your clipboard, a .txt file. The location check compares your coordinates against map files served as part of this page, on your device.

        If you use one-click delivery, your letter passes through our relay exactly once: it lives in memory for the duration of the send, is handed to the Senate's mail server, and is gone. No copy is written to disk, mail logging is disabled, and the relay cannot reconstruct a letter after the fact. You're CC'd, so the only durable copies are yours and the committee's. The code that does this is in the public repo.

        How does one-click sending work, and why does it want my email?

        On a self-hosted copy, the send button hands your reviewed letter to a small relay, which logs in to a mail service (a free account the operator set up) and sends it to the committee with your name on it. It goes from that service's mailbox, not "from" your address: sending as you without your mail provider's keys is how phishing works, it would be rejected, and the relay refuses to forge it. Your address rides as Reply-To and CC instead, so the office replies straight to you and you keep a copy. The email requirement is deliberate: offices discount mail nobody can answer, and a real reply address is what makes this delivery, not spam. Three sends per hour per connection, no anonymous letters, no exceptions. (This GitHub-hosted copy runs no relay, so the button uses your own mail app instead.)

        I don't live in California. Is writing worth my time?

        Yes, with honest expectations. Letters from constituents carry the most weight, and out-of-state letters carry less. They are still counted as public support, and this bill sets the rules for games sold everywhere, because publishers do not build separate versions for one state. Your channels are the position letter portal and the committee inbox. If you want maximum effect, also send the cheat sheet to a friend who lives in California.

        Isn't this just astroturf with extra steps?

        No, and the difference is enforceable: nothing here sends anything. You pick the points, you add the story only you have, you edit the draft, and you send it from your own email account or portal login under your own name. The reworder exists so that two people who picked the same points don't produce the same paragraph, not to fake a movement that isn't there. The movement part is you bothering to do this.

        Why letters and calls instead of a petition?

        A petition with half a million signatures enters the record as one item. Fifty individual letters enter as fifty, each with a name and a city, and staff tally them ahead of the hearing. Calls get logged the same way. This is the format the building counts.

        What does AB 1921 actually require?

        For buy-to-play games first sold or re-released on or after January 1, 2027: tell players at least 60 days before shutting down the services the game needs, then provide one of three things, publisher's choice: a standalone version, a patch that lets the game run without the operator's servers, or a full refund. Subscription games, free-to-play games, and permanent DRM-free downloads are exempt. Read the bill text.
